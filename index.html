Module 00

Foundations: the language and the loop

Before tools, agree on what the words mean. Most GTM programs fail because ICP, segment, lead, and account are used interchangeably.
Learning objective
Explain ICP, account, contact, buying committee, trigger, and lane in one sentence each.
Learning objective
Describe the seven-stage loop: define, discover, enrich, store, route, activate, measure.
Learning objective
Judge when a list is research and when it is just noise.
Functional process

Set the ground rules

  1. Write one-line definitions for ICP, account, persona, trigger, lane, and suppression.
  2. Draw your current funnel and mark where accounts enter today.
  3. Identify the systems of record you already own (CRM, MAP, spreadsheets).
  4. Agree who approves an ICP change and who owns data quality.
  5. Set a single evidence standard: every claim needs a source URL and date.
Use case sample

Team with 40,000 unused contacts

A 300-person B2B software firm buys a 40k contact list and emails everyone. Reply rate 0.2%, domain reputation drops.

  • Diagnosis: no ICP, no scoring, no evidence.
  • Fix path: define ICP, keep 1,800 accounts, suppress the rest.
Tools & AI

Starter stack

  • CRM: HubSpot or Salesforce (system of record).
  • Docs: a written ICP one-pager, versioned.
  • Spreadsheet or Airtable for the first 100 accounts.
  • AI: GPT-class / Claude / Gemini for definitions, summarizing, and challenge-my-assumption prompts.
  • Guardrail: AI drafts, a human approves.
Exit criteria: You can explain the full loop on a whiteboard and name the owner of each stage.
Module 00 · Foundations03
Module 01

ICP definition and research

The ICP is a decision rule, not a description. It must be specific enough that two people looking at the same company reach the same verdict.
Learning objective
Build an ICP from evidence: won deals, lost deals, churn, and market data.
Learning objective
Express fit as firmographics + complexity + pain + trigger + reachability.
Learning objective
Write explicit exclusions so the team can say no quickly.
Functional process

Define who is worth researching

  1. Analyse your last 20 wins and 20 losses: what is common in the wins?
  2. Draft fit dimensions: industry, employee band, geography, revenue, business model.
  3. Add complexity signals: multiple systems, brands, regions, acquisitions.
  4. Add problem fit: the pain you actually solve, in the customer's words.
  5. Add trigger types and exclusions (no owner, no pain, no budget, wrong motion).
  6. Validate with 10 real companies before you publish it.
Use case sample

RevOps consolidation ICP

B2B services and software, 200–5,000 employees, 2+ CRMs or a recent acquisition, a named RevOps or data owner, EU/UK/US.

  • Exclude: <50 employees, no operations leader, single-system startups.
  • Trigger types: new RevOps hire, merger, platform migration, funding.
Tools & AI

Research and validation

  • Sources: CRM win/loss data, Crunchbase, industry reports, customer interviews.
  • Market sizing: Sales Navigator or Apollo filter counts to test if the ICP is big enough.
  • AI: cluster your won-account descriptions, extract shared attributes, draft an ICP hypothesis with cited evidence.
  • AI prompt pattern: "Here are 20 won and 20 lost accounts, what distinguishes them? List evidence, flag guesses."
Exit criteria: A one-page ICP with fit dimensions, triggers, exclusions, and an addressable count you believe.

Module 02

Account scoring and tiering

Turn the ICP into a number so resourcing is a rule, not an opinion. Pain and timing should outweigh company size.
Scoring model · 100 points

Firmographic fit 20 · Data or process complexity 20 · Problem fit 20 · Marketing/RevOps maturity 15 · Active trigger 15 · Reachability 10

Score only from recorded evidence. No evidence, no points.

Tiers drive investment

Tier A 80–100 — ABM, executive-led, 1:1 research, human sequencing.

Tier B 60–79 — personalized outbound and 1:few nurture.

Tier C 40–59 — drip education, wait for a trigger.

Below 40 — suppress and revisit quarterly.

Tools & AI
  • Score fields and calculated properties in HubSpot / Salesforce.
  • PostgreSQL or a warehouse for weight changes and back-testing.
  • Clay tables for scoring during enrichment.
  • AI: classify free-text evidence into score dimensions, flag unsupported points, explain each score in one sentence.
Worked exampleEvidencePointsOutcome
Nordic logistics group1,800 employees, 3 acquired brands, 2 CRMs, new VP RevOps (Q2), Salesforce + MarketoFit 18 · Complexity 20 · Problem 18 · Maturity 12 · Trigger 15 · Reach 8 = 91Tier A → ABM + trigger outbound
Regional SaaS, 90 staffSingle HubSpot instance, no ops owner, no triggerFit 8 · Complexity 4 · Problem 6 · Maturity 6 · Trigger 0 · Reach 6 = 30Suppress → quarterly re-check
Exit criteria: every account in your list carries a score, a tier, and a one-line reason a human can audit.

Module 03

Account research

Company research produces a hypothesis, not a row. For each account you need a problem you believe they have and the evidence that says so.
Learning objective
Build an account universe from filters, then narrow it with evidence.
Learning objective
Recognise the seven trigger families that make outreach timely.
Learning objective
Record source, date, and confidence for every claim you make.
Functional process

Research workflow

  1. Build the universe: industry, size, geography, tech, growth filters.
  2. Layer signals: hiring, funding, leadership change, M&A, migration, expansion, regulation.
  3. Read the primary sources: site, careers page, product docs, annual report, reviews.
  4. Write a two-sentence pain hypothesis in the customer's language.
  5. Attach evidence URLs, date, and a confidence level (high/medium/low).
  6. Score, tier, and hand off — or reject with a reason.
Use case sample

New RevOps leader after an acquisition

A 1,200-person company hires a VP RevOps three months after acquiring a competitor; job posts mention "CRM consolidation".

  • Hypothesis: duplicate accounts, conflicting owners, broken lifecycle stages.
  • Evidence: job ad, press release, two CRM tech signals.
  • Route: Tier A, ABM + trigger outbound, CTA = reconciliation workshop.
Tools & AI

Research stack

  • Universe: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, ZoomInfo, Clay.
  • Signals: Crunchbase, job boards, BuiltWith/HG Insights, news alerts, G2.
  • Capture: Clay tables or PostgreSQL research table with source columns.
  • AI: summarize 10-K/press/careers pages, classify trigger type, draft the hypothesis, and list what evidence is missing.
  • Guardrail: AI may summarize sources; it may not invent them.
Exit criteria: A scored account list where each row has trigger, hypothesis, evidence links, and confidence.

Module 04

Enrichment, identity and data hygiene

Personalization built on dirty data is worse than no personalization. Clean identity first, then enrich, then activate.
Learning objective
Normalize a company to one canonical account with parents and subsidiaries.
Learning objective
Set validation rules for domains, duplicates, and email verification.
Learning objective
Decide what AI may infer and what must be deterministic.
Functional process

Hygiene process

  1. Resolve identity: canonical name + primary domain as the join key.
  2. Model the account family: parent, subsidiary, region — keep local buying units.
  3. Enrich firmographics, tech stack, and headcount from 2+ sources.
  4. Deduplicate with match rules, then merge with an audit trail.
  5. Verify emails; mark catch-all and risky addresses.
  6. Stamp every field with source, date, and confidence; expire stale data.
Use case sample

Three records, one customer

"Acme Ltd", "ACME Group UK", and "Acme Logistics" exist as three accounts with three owners.

  • Merge into one account family on domain; keep the UK subsidiary as a child.
  • Result: no duplicate outreach, one owner, one score.
Tools & AI

Enrichment stack

  • Clay, Apollo, ZoomInfo, Cognism for firmographic and contact data.
  • NeverBounce / ZeroBounce for email verification.
  • PostgreSQL matching tables or dbt models for dedupe logic.
  • Python for domain resolution and fuzzy matching.
  • AI: normalize industry text, classify company type, spot conflicting values — deterministic rules still decide.
Exit criteria: One trusted account record per real company, with evidence history and a confidence field.

Module 05

People research and the buying committee

You do not sell to a company, you sell to four to seven people with different fears. Map them before you write a single email.
Learning objective
Identify economic buyer, champion, evaluator, user, and blocker.
Learning objective
Match each role to the value they care about and the proof they need.
Learning objective
Apply consent and compliance rules to contact data.
Functional process

People workflow

  1. Confirm the account is Tier A/B before researching people.
  2. Map roles by function, seniority, and region — not by title alone.
  3. Find 3–6 contacts covering economic, champion, technical, and user roles.
  4. Verify role from LinkedIn, site, talks, or posts; record the source.
  5. Note each person's likely priority and objection.
  6. Verify email; check consent basis and suppression before enrollment.
Use case sample

RevOps consolidation committee

One account, four people, four different messages from one hypothesis.

  • CRO — pipeline visibility and forecast risk.
  • VP RevOps (champion) — ownership, lifecycle, routing.
  • CRM architect — integration and migration proof.
  • Marketing ops (user) — day-to-day workflow relief.
Tools & AI

People stack

  • Sales Navigator, Apollo, ZoomInfo, Cognism, Lusha.
  • Clay waterfall enrichment for coverage; email verification for safety.
  • CRM contact roles / buying-committee field to store the map.
  • AI: infer likely committee roles from an org's job titles, draft a one-line priority per person, spot committee gaps.
  • Compliance: GDPR/CAN-SPAM lawful basis, opt-out, and regional rules.
Exit criteria: Each Tier A/B account has a mapped, verified, compliant buying committee.

Module 06

The data layer: where everything lives

Separate the evidence you researched, the truth you trust, and the record you activate. Mixing them destroys either your audit trail or your campaigns.
Raw layer

Research & sources

Source URLs, snapshots, enrichment responses, timestamps, raw scrapes. Never edited.

→
Trusted layer

Normalized accounts & people

Canonical identity, score, tier, trigger, committee, confidence, owner.

→
Activation layer

CRM & campaigns

Lifecycle stage, lane, enrollment, tasks, suppression, handoffs.

Execution system

HubSpot / Salesforce

Accounts, contacts, owners, activities, lifecycle, campaign enrollment, suppression lists.

Operational system

PostgreSQL / Airtable / Clay

Research history, dedupe logic, score calculations, trigger event log, reconciliation.

Analytics system

Snowflake · BigQuery · Power BI

Cross-source analysis, cohort and lane performance, attribution, pipeline reporting.

Use case: a campaign changes lifecycle stage in the activation layer without touching the research evidence that justified the score — so you can still explain, six months later, why the account was Tier A. Exit criteria: you can trace any campaign enrollment back to its source URL.

Module 08

Campaign types: drip, nurture, ABM and friends

Same account universe, different intent. The difference is who chooses the content — the calendar, the behaviour, or the account team.
Time-based · 1:many

Drip

Fixed sequence on a schedule, low personalization, education first. 5–7 emails over 6 weeks.
Use for: Tier C, new subscribers, early awareness.
Sample: "Five signs your CRM data is blocking forecasting" series.
Tools: HubSpot sequences/workflows, Mailchimp, Instantly. AI: variant copy, subject-line testing.

Behaviour-based · 1:few

Nurture

Branches on what the contact does; moves problem → approach → proof → offer.
Use for: Tier B engaged, post-webinar, demo no-shows.
Sample: pricing-page visit branches into an ROI calculator and a case study.
Tools: HubSpot / Marketo / Customer.io, lead scoring. AI: next-best-content, intent classification.

Account-based · 1:1

ABM

Account team targets the whole committee with a shared narrative and coordinated channels.
Use for: Tier A, named accounts, expansion.
Sample: CRO, RevOps and architect each get a role-specific asset plus a joint workshop invite.
Tools: HubSpot ABM, Demandbase, 6sense, LinkedIn ads, direct mail. AI: account dossier, role-specific drafts.

Event-based · fast

Trigger outbound

Fires within days of a signal: funding, hire, migration, acquisition, job post.
Use for: any tier with a fresh, credible trigger.
Sample: new VP RevOps → sales task + 3-step personalized sequence in 48 hours.
Tools: n8n/Make, Clay webhooks, CRM tasks, Slack alerts. AI: signal summary, first-line drafting.

Re-engagement

Dormant 6–12 months. Short, honest, opt-in refresh, then suppress non-responders to protect deliverability.

Onboarding & lifecycle

Post-sale activation, adoption, expansion — same machinery, customer data instead of research data.

Event / webinar

Workshop or roundtable as the CTA for Tier A. Invite, remind, follow up by attendance behaviour.

Module 09

Message, content and the CTA ladder

One account hypothesis becomes four messages. Personalization means relevance to a role's problem — not merging a first name.
Learning objective
Translate a pain hypothesis into role-specific value statements.
Learning objective
Build a CTA ladder from low commitment to a paid engagement.
Learning objective
Set a proof standard so no claim ships without evidence.
Functional process

Message build

  1. State the account problem in one sentence, sourced from evidence.
  2. Write the value per role: executive = risk/growth, champion = process, technical = architecture, user = daily relief.
  3. Choose proof: case study, benchmark, teardown, calculator, or checklist.
  4. Ladder the CTA: subscribe → guide → assessment → workshop → proposal.
  5. Sequence channels: email, LinkedIn, ads, call, event.
  6. Review: is every claim supported? Remove anything unverifiable.
Use case sample

One hypothesis, four emails

Trigger: acquisition + new RevOps leader. Shared narrative: "two CRMs are costing you forecast accuracy".

  • CRO: forecast risk in the first board cycle post-merger.
  • RevOps: a 30-day consolidation sequence and ownership model.
  • Architect: integration pattern and data-governance controls.
  • Ops user: fewer manual merges each week.
Tools & AI

Content & AI

  • HubSpot Marketing Hub or Marketo for assets, landing pages, and forms.
  • LinkedIn for role targeting; webinar tooling for workshop CTAs.
  • AI: draft role variants from the account dossier, tone checks, and translation.
  • AI: score your own draft — "list every claim and whether the evidence supports it".
  • Never let AI generate a metric, customer name, or quote.
Exit criteria: A message matrix: role × problem × proof × CTA, all traceable to evidence.

Automation, orchestration and the AI layer

Automation makes the routing table run itself. AI accelerates research and drafting inside guardrails — it never approves its own output.

AI you will actually use

Chat/reasoning models for research summaries and drafts · embeddings for clustering and similarity · agents in Clay or Copilot Studio for repeatable lookups · transcription for call insight.

Guardrails

Human approval before send · sources required for every claim · confidence thresholds · no AI-invented numbers or names · log prompts and outputs · review sampled output weekly.

Build order

Manual for 20 accounts → semi-automated with AI drafts → automated ingest and routing → agents only for steps you have already run by hand successfully.

Module 11

Measure, diagnose and improve

Measure the machine, not just the campaign. Most bad results come from research and routing errors, not from the email.
Learning objective
Track coverage, engagement, and pipeline by lane and by tier.
Learning objective
Diagnose whether a failure is data, routing, message, or timing.
Learning objective
Feed learnings back into the score weights and the ICP.
Functional process

Improvement loop

  1. Measure: account coverage, committee coverage, reply, meeting, pipeline, win rate — split by lane and tier.
  2. Compare lanes: is Tier A ABM outperforming Tier B outbound per account?
  3. Diagnose failures: missing fields? wrong lane? weak proof? stale trigger?
  4. Adjust one variable at a time; keep a change log.
  5. Re-weight the score using accounts that actually converted.
  6. Retire dead segments and re-publish the ICP quarterly.
Use case sample

Drip beats ABM — why?

Tier A ABM produces fewer meetings than the Tier C drip in month two.

  • Check: committee coverage was 1.2 contacts per Tier A account.
  • Root cause is people research depth, not the campaign.
  • Fix in Module 05, not by rewriting emails.
Tools & AI

Measurement stack

  • CRM dashboards for lane, tier, and pipeline reporting.
  • Warehouse + Power BI / Looker for cohort and attribution analysis.
  • Data-quality KPIs: completeness, duplicate rate, correction rate, bounce rate.
  • AI KPIs: research hours saved, accepted recommendations, QA exception rate.
  • AI: cluster lost reasons and reply text to surface message themes.
Exit criteria: A monthly review where score weights, lanes, and the ICP are updated from evidence.

Delivery plan

Six-week schedule and capstone

Two modules per week, each with a live exercise on the learner's own market. The capstone is a working mini-system, not a slide deck.
Week 1Modules 00–01
Exercise: write the ICP one-pager and validate it against 10 real companies.
Week 2Modules 02–03
Exercise: score 30 accounts and research 10 with full evidence and triggers.
Week 3Modules 04–05
Exercise: clean and dedupe the 30, map committees on the top 10.
Week 4Modules 06–07
Exercise: build the data layer and write the routing table as workflow logic.
Week 5Modules 08–09
Exercise: design one drip, one nurture, one ABM play, one trigger sequence.
Week 6Modules 10–11
Exercise: automate ingest and routing, build the dashboard, run the first review.
Capstone deliverable

A live mini-GTM system

ICP one-pager · 30 scored accounts · 10 researched with evidence · committees mapped · routing table implemented · four campaign lanes built · one dashboard.

Assessment

Can it be audited?

Pick any enrolled contact at random: the learner must show the source, the score, the trigger, the lane rule, and the CTA — in under two minutes.

Common failure modes

Researching people before qualifying accounts · scoring on size alone · one message for the whole committee · no suppression · AI output shipped unreviewed.
